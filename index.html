Second Amendment News

  • ATF Stops Enforcing NFA Registration on Suppressors, SBRs, SBSs, and AOWs Nationwide
    Effective Oct. 9, the ATF will no longer require NFA approval or registration before making or transferring short-barreled rifles, short-barreled shotguns, suppressors, or 'any other weapons,' following a Texas court ruling and last year's tax repeal in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Form 4473 and NICS checks through an FFL are still required.
    Investigate further: yes, directly affects range/training business operations since customers will start asking about buying suppressors and SBRs without the old registration wait
  • Supreme Court to Hear AR-15 Ban Challenge Viramontes v. Cook County Dec. 2
    The Court will decide whether the Second and Fourteenth Amendments protect the right to own AR-15-platform rifles, consolidating challenges to Cook County, Illinois and Connecticut's assault weapons bans. The DOJ has already filed a brief siding with the challengers; a ruling is expected by June 2027.
    Investigate further: yes, a ruling either way will directly shape what rifles are legal to sell, rent, or train with nationwide
  • DOJ Opens Civil Rights Probe Into NJ Township Over Gun Permit Delays
    The Justice Department's Second Amendment Section is investigating whether Pennsauken Township, NJ police used 'egregious and unjust' delays issuing firearm permits to effectively deny residents their carry rights, despite a state-mandated 30-day turnaround. The probe leans on the Bruen standard against de facto denial through red tape.
    Investigate further: no, a local NJ enforcement issue, but worth a mental bookmark since it's the kind of permit-delay precedent that could get cited elsewhere

Verbal Judo

Tip: When someone comes in hot, don't match their volume or correct them right out of the gate — open with an empathetic paraphrase ('Sounds like you've been waiting a while and that's frustrating') before you redirect. It costs you nothing and it takes their legs out from under the fight they walked in ready to have.

Exercise: Today, pick one tense interaction before it happens (a late vendor, an impatient new shooter waiting on a lane, one of the kids pushing back on a chore) and force yourself to say one genuine empathy line out loud before you say anything else. Notice how much faster it de-escalates versus your default response.
