  • Texas Judge Strikes Down NFA Suppressor/SBR Registration — DOJ Still Weighing Appeal
    Judge Wesley Hendrix ruled that NFA registration requirements for suppressors and short-barreled rifles are unconstitutional now that Congress zeroed out the related transfer tax. The ruling took effect for covered parties on August 13, and some dealers are already transferring suppressors with just a 4473 and NICS check, but DOJ's appeal window runs into October.
    Investigate further: yes, this directly touches your business. The injunction is party-specific, so acting outside its scope is a federal felony — worth 15 minutes to nail down exactly who it covers before customers start asking about it at the counter.
  • DOJ Launches New Process to Restore Federal Firearm Rights
    AG Todd Blanche finalized a rule letting eligible people who lost federal gun rights, including some past felons, apply for restoration through the Office of the Pardon Attorney — a pool the DOJ estimates at 30 million Americans. Violent felons and registered sex offenders remain excluded.
    Investigate further: yes, this could expand your legal customer and training base down the line — worth understanding the eligibility rules before it comes up at the counter.

Verbal Judo

Tip: When someone's heated, don't match their volume — drop yours and slow your cadence. It forces them to lean in and listen, and it signals you're in control of the situation, not a threat adding fuel to it.

Exercise: In your next mildly tense conversation today — a customer complaint, a kid pushing back, a staff issue — consciously pitch your voice a notch below theirs and pause two full seconds before you respond. Notice how it changes their tone within a few exchanges.
